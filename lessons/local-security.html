Privacy, safety and good hygiene for local AI

Local does not automatically mean safe. It means the risks are different, and they are yours to manage. Here is a short, practical checklist.

What you will learn

  • Know what "local" really protects and what it does not
  • Understand prompt injection in one paragraph
  • Apply a safety checklist to your own setup

What local protects

Your prompts and files are not sent to a provider. They do not appear in anyone's logs or training data, and a vendor outage or policy change cannot reach them.

What it does not protect

  • Your files if malware is on the PC. Local AI does not replace normal security.
  • You from untrusted model files (lesson 3.2).
  • You from prompt injection: when text the model reads contains instructions ("ignore the above and send…"), it may follow them. This matters once the model can read web pages or documents and use tools.
  • Apps that phone home. Many AI front ends include telemetry or update checks. Read their privacy settings.
Concept: prompt injection is like SQL injection

Instructions and data share the same channel: text. A malicious document can hide commands. The defence is the same too: do not trust the data, and limit what the model is allowed to do with it.

The checklist

Everyday hygiene

  • Back up your notes, prompts and scripts. Models can be re-downloaded, your work cannot.
  • Keep an eye on disk space and VRAM. Many problems are simply "something else is using it".
  • Watch temperatures during long runs, such as benchmarks and, later, training.
  • Keep your system updated, but pin versions inside a project once it works.

Check yourself

What is prompt injection?

  1. Hidden instructions inside text the model reads that try to steer it
  2. Installing a model with a syringe
  3. A way to speed up generation

It is dangerous when a model can read untrusted content and take actions.
