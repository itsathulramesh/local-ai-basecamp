Supervised fine-tuning teaches by example. Some goals need other kinds of training: preferring good answers over bad ones, copying a stronger model, reading more domain text, or practising tasks with checkable answers. This lesson is your map of those methods.

What you will learn

  • Know when SFT stops being enough
  • Explain DPO, distillation, continued pretraining and GRPO
  • Know what is realistic on a home GPU

Where SFT stops

SFT shows the model one good answer per prompt. It cannot express "this answer is better than that one", and it cannot reward the model for finding a correct answer by itself. That is where preference and reinforcement methods come in.

DPO: better versus worse

Direct Preference Optimization trains on pairs: for the same prompt, a chosen answer and a rejected one. The model is pushed toward the chosen style and away from the rejected one, without training a separate reward model. It is the same family of methods that gives chat models their manners, and it is also used to "heal" quality after abliteration (lesson 10.3).

{"prompt": "Explain VRAM briefly.", "chosen": "VRAM is the graphics card's own memory...", "rejected": "Great question! As an AI, I'd be happy to..."}

TRL provides DPOTrainer and Unsloth supports it with LoRA, so DPO on small models fits the same GPUs as SFT. Related methods you will see named: ORPO (combines SFT and preference in one step) and KTO (needs only "good" or "bad" labels, not pairs).

Distillation: borrow a stronger model's skill

A big teacher answers many prompts and a small student is fine-tuned on those answers. The student picks up much of the teacher's style and some of its skill at a fraction of the size. Your dataset script in lesson 9.2 is a simple form of this. Check the teacher's licence or terms for conditions on training with its outputs.

Continued pretraining: more reading

Instead of chat examples, train on raw domain text (the {"text": ...} format) so the model absorbs vocabulary and style of a field, such as medical notes or a programming language. It needs far more data and compute than SFT, and it is usually followed by an SFT step to restore assistant behaviour.

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (GRPO)

For tasks where you can check an answer automatically (maths with a known result, code that must pass tests, output that must be valid JSON), the model generates several attempts, a reward function scores each, and the model is nudged toward the higher-scoring ones. GRPO is the popular method behind recent reasoning models. Unsloth supports it for some small models, but it is slower and fussier than SFT. Try it after you are comfortable with SFT.

Model merging

Combine two fine-tunes of the same base into one model without further training. Covered in lesson 10.5.
