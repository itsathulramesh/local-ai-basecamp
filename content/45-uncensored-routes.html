You now have every piece: fine-tuning, adapters, and weight editing. This lesson puts them together into practical recipes for a model that is both yours (trained on your data and style) and free of the refusals that get in your way.

What you will learn

  • Pick the right route for your hardware and goal
  • Evaluate task quality and refusals separately
  • Handle licences and responsibility

The five routes

Route 1 in practice

Community publishers such as huihui-ai release abliterated versions of popular models as safetensors in several sizes. One example is huihui-ai/Huihui-Qwen3.5-9B-abliterated (Apache-2.0). Search Hugging Face for "abliterated" plus your model family and size, check the card names its base model and method, then put it in train_lora.py:

MODEL = "huihui-ai/SOME-MODEL-abliterated"   # must be safetensors, not GGUF
# everything else in train_lora.py stays the same

You can never train on a GGUF-only release. If a model is published only as GGUF, use route 4 or 5 for it.

Route 3: training data that does not refuse

Research by Qi et al. (2023) showed that fine-tuning on as few as 10 non-refusing examples could strip much of a model's safety behaviour, and that even ordinary benign fine-tuning erodes it somewhat. In practice, for your own model:

  • Write examples in your domain where the ideal answer is complete and direct, with no moralising or disclaimers, for requests the base model hedged on.
  • Keep them correct and high quality. Every flaw is learned too.
  • Mix them with your normal task examples so the model learns your style and your directness together.

Route 4 in practice

# Ollama Modelfile
FROM ./Some-Model-9B-Uncensored-Q4_K_M.gguf
ADAPTER ./my-lora-F16.gguf      # trained on the OFFICIAL Some-Model-9B

An adapter learns a difference from the base it was trained on. A derivative made with small weight edits (such as abliteration) is close enough that it often works, but it is an empirical question. Compare against the same model without the adapter.

Fine-tuning can bring refusals back

An adapter trained on the official base learned in the presence of its refusal behaviour, and may partly restore it when applied to an uncensored model. Equally, fine-tuning an abliterated model on cautious data can re-teach caution. Measure, do not assume.

Choosing a route

  • Want the quickest result? Route 5, then route 4.
  • Want the cleanest custom model? Route 1.
  • Want to understand and control everything? Route 2.
  • Only one domain is over-refused? Route 3.

Licences and responsibility

  • Derivatives inherit the base model's licence. Some restrict certain uses or require attribution. Read it before you share a model.
  • If you publish an uncensored model, document the base, the method and your evaluation honestly, as you would want others to.
  • The law applies to what you produce and do with the output, not the tool (lesson 5.3). Removing refusals moves all of the judgement to you.
  • Keep unrestricted models local, bound to localhost, and away from anything other people can use.
