Two models can both fit and still feel completely different to use. Speed comes from memory bandwidth and model size, and you can estimate it before you download anything.

What you will learn

  • Distinguish prompt processing speed from generation speed
  • Estimate tokens per second from bandwidth and model size
  • Understand why mixture-of-experts models feel fast
  • Know what "good" speed feels like

Two speeds

  • Prompt processing (also called prefill or time to first token): reading your whole prompt. It is compute-heavy and fast on a GPU, usually hundreds to thousands of tokens per second.
  • Generation (decoding): producing the answer one token at a time. This is what "tokens per second" usually means, and it is limited by memory bandwidth.

The ceiling formula

Concept: bandwidth ÷ model size

Generating one token reads all the active weights once. So tokens per second can never exceed memory bandwidth divided by the size of the weights read. A card with 500 GB/s running a 5 GB model has a ceiling of about 100 tokens per second. Real speed is usually 55 to 75% of that.

Why mixture-of-experts feels fast

A 35B-A3B model reads only about 3 billion active parameters per token, so its per-token cost resembles a 3B model while its answers resemble a much larger one. The price is that all 35B must still be stored. On a 24 GB card that is a great trade.

What is "fast enough"?

Tokens per secondFeel
Under 5Painful. Usually a sign of CPU spill.
5 to 15About reading speed. Usable for short answers.
20 to 40Comfortable for chat
50 and aboveFeels instant. Great for agents and bulk jobs.
Thinking models are slower than their speed suggests

Reasoning models write a long hidden chain of thought before the answer. At 30 tokens per second, 2,000 tokens of thinking is over a minute. You can usually shorten or disable thinking in the settings.

Measure your own

LM Studio shows tokens per second under each reply, and Ollama prints it with ollama run MODEL --verbose. Record it in your notes next to the model, quantization and context length. Over the weeks you will build a personal table of what your PC does.

Check yourself

A GPU has 1,000 GB/s bandwidth and the model weights are 10 GB. What is the speed ceiling?

  1. About 100 tokens per second
  2. About 10,000 tokens per second
  3. About 10 tokens per second

1,000 ÷ 10 = 100. Real speed will be somewhat lower.

Why is a 30B-A3B mixture-of-experts model usually much faster than a 30B dense model?

  1. It uses less memory in total
  2. It reads only the active experts for each token
  3. It does not need a GPU

Total memory is the same or larger, but per-token reads are small.
